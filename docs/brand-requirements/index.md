Brand is not decoration. It tells a user which part of government they are
dealing with and whether they can trust what they are reading. Get the brand
category right before you make any visual decision.

## Brand architecture at a glance

| Category | Use when | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Master brand | The service is delivered by the Queensland Government as a whole | Coat of Arms, no agency mark |
| Sub-brand | An agency or program with its own audience, still clearly government | Coat of Arms plus a program name |
| Co-brand | Delivered jointly with another government or organisation | Two marks of equal weight |
| Endorsed | A distinct identity that government supports | Its own mark, with an endorsement line |
| Standalone | A separate entity with no visible government identity | No Coat of Arms |

If you cannot tell which category applies, the answer is almost always **master
brand**. Departing from it needs a decision on the record.

## What is fixed

Three things are the same in every category that shows the Coat of Arms:

1. **The Coat of Arms itself.** Never recolour, redraw, crop or add effects to it.
2. **The header and footer structure.** Use `qgds-header` and `qgds-footer` as
   supplied.
3. **The acknowledgement of Country.** It belongs in the footer of every
   Queensland Government service.
